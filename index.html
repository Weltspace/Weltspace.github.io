Research科研

Locally Private Subgraph Counting via Noisy Adjacency Matrix and Differential Privacy on Randomized Data
Jintao Guo, Ying Zhou, GuiXun Luo, Chao Li, Yan Hu
Under review USENIX Security 2027 · Preprint on arXiv since July 2025
[arXiv] [Code (anonymized for review)]

Subgraph counting (triangles, 4-cycles) is a fundamental primitive in graph analytics, yet existing edge-LDP protocols force a harsh trade-off between privacy trust assumptions, accuracy, and communication cost. I lead the design of a dual-framework architecture — Noisy Adjacency Matrix (NAM) and Differential Privacy on Randomized Data (DPRD) — that achieves strict edge-LDP without any trusted server or shuffler.

  • Frameworks. NAM reformulates private subgraph counting as algebraic estimation over randomized matrices; DPRD resolves the privacy-composition challenge when a later-round mechanism takes randomized data as input.
  • Algorithms. One- and two-round estimators for triangle and quadrangle counting: TriOR, TriMTR, QuaTR, and TriTR*.
  • Theory. The first exact closed-form MSE for the RR-based one-round triangle estimator class, and the first relative-error guarantee for private subgraph counting — error does not grow with graph size when the clustering coefficient and average degree are stable.
  • Experiments. At ε = 1, TriMTR and QuaTR reduce relative error to 0.0198×–0.0066× and 0.1963×–0.4407× of the strongest baselines (up to ~150× more accurate), outperforming all evaluated edge-LDP and shuffle-model methods.
  • My role. Problem formulation, algorithm design, theoretical proofs, implementation, experiments, and paper writing, as first author and project leader of a National-Level Undergraduate Innovation Training Program (top 3 of 130 projects at completion).
